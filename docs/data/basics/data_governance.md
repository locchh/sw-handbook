# Data Governance & Management

**Data management** is the whole practice of handling data as an asset across its [lifecycle](introduction_to_data.md). **Governance** is the layer above it: the policies, roles, and decision rights that say *who* owns data, *who* may use it, and *how* those calls get made. [Introduction to Data](introduction_to_data.md) named governance as a core concept; this page is the operating model.

## What It Is

Governance answers ownership and accountability through defined roles:

| Role | Owns | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Data Owner** | a domain (accountable) | approves access, sets policy |
| **Data Steward** | day-to-day quality & meaning | defines terms, resolves issues |
| **Data Custodian** | the technical store | runs infra, applies controls |

Its deliverables are the connective tissue of a data org: a **business glossary** (agreed definitions), a **data catalog** (what exists, where), **lineage** (where it came from), and **policies** (retention, access, privacy).

## Frameworks That Apply

- **DAMA-DMBOK** — the *Data Management Body of Knowledge*; its wheel of 11 areas (governance at the hub, plus quality, architecture, security, metadata…) is the field's canonical map.
- **DCAM** (EDM Council) & **CMMI DMM** — capability-maturity models to assess *how good* your practice is and where to invest next.
- **DGI Data Governance Framework** — a practical model of rules, people, and processes.
- **COBIT** — IT governance that situates data governance inside enterprise control.

## Golden Lesson

**Governance is people and process, not a tool.** Start by assigning owners and stewards, then write policy — a policy with no accountable owner is a document nobody follows. And keep it proportionate: govern enough to be trustworthy and compliant, not so much that it strangles the work.
